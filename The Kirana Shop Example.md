## Part 4 — The number that changed everything

### First, forget options. Think of a shop.

You run a small kirana shop. On every packet you sell, you make **₹6 profit**.

But sometimes a customer walks out without paying. Each time that happens, you lose **₹35** worth of goods.

Now — **how many honest customers do you need for every one thief, just to stay flat?**

```
₹35 loss  ÷  ₹6 profit  =  5.83
```

So you need about **6 honest customers to cover 1 thief.**

That means out of every 7 people who walk in, 6 must be honest:

```
6 out of 7  =  85%
```

**85% of your customers must be honest just for you to break even.** Not to profit. To be flat.

If 8 out of 10 people are honest — a shop most of us would call excellent — **you are losing money.**

### That is exactly the options trade

| Shop | Options |
|---|---|
| ₹6 profit per sale | ₹6 you book on a winning trade |
| ₹35 lost to a thief | ₹35 you lose when the trade goes bad |
| 85% honest customers needed | 85% win rate needed |

Same arithmetic. Different clothes.

---

### The one line worth memorising

```
Break-even win rate  =        loss
                        ---------------
                        win  +  loss
```

That's it. Put your own numbers in.

```
      35
   --------   =  0.85   →   85%
   6  +  35
```

---

### Now let's fix the shop

**Fix 1 — Install a CCTV.** Now you catch the thief early. Instead of ₹35 of goods, he only gets away with ₹15.

```
      15
   --------   =  0.71   →   71%
   6  +  15
```

You went from needing 85% to needing 71%. **You didn't sell one extra packet.** You just stopped the losses from getting big.

**Fix 2 — Raise your margin slightly.** Now you make ₹7.50 per sale instead of ₹6.

```
     15
  ----------   =  0.67   →   67%
  7.5  +  15
```

**67%.**

---

### Look at what actually moved the needle

| What I changed | Win rate I need |
|---|---|
| Nothing | **85%** |
| Cut the loss (CCTV) | **71%** |
| Cut the loss + better margin | **67%** |

Getting from 85% to 67% didn't come from finding better customers. It came from **controlling what happens when things go wrong.**

### Read this twice

> **Your exit rule decides how accurate you need to be. Not your entry.**

Every one of us spends our life on entry. Which indicator. Which candle. Which support level. Which YouTube setup.

Meanwhile the exit rule was quietly deciding whether the whole thing could ever work — before we placed a single trade.

And I had never done this calculation for any strategy I had traded in my life. Not once.

---

### Do this right now, before you read further

Take whatever strategy you are trading today. Answer two questions:

1. When it works, how much do I make?
2. When it fails, how much do I lose?

Put them in the formula. Then ask yourself honestly — **am I actually that good?**

Most people have never checked. That is the whole problem.
